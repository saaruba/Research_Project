## Notes on specific entries — read before submitting

1. **Repiso, Garrell and Sanfeliu — cited as (2019) throughout your drafts, but Springer's official print volume is dated 2020** (12(4), pp. 909–930, print August 2020; accepted/online May 2019). I've kept the in-text year consistent with what's already used across your four chapters rather than silently changing it, but Harvard style technically wants the year of the version you're citing — if you cite the page numbers above, the more defensible year is **2020**. Pick one and make sure Chapters 2, 3, and 1 all match this list — an in-text year that doesn't match the reference list is one of the most common, and most easily caught, referencing errors a marker checks for.
2. **Pagès, Marchionni and Ferro (TIAGo paper)** — I could not get a page that explicitly states the exact workshop name/location beyond what's already well-documented publicly (an IROS 2016-associated Workshop on Robot Modularity, hosted via clawar.org). This is a very widely cited, easy-to-verify paper — a 30-second Google Scholar check on your end will confirm or correct the venue string before submission.
3. **Kendon (1990)** is a book, not a paper — no page range applies to the whole-work citation above; if you quote a specific passage anywhere in Chapter 2, add the specific page number to that in-text citation only.
4. **Wang et al. and Yaseen** are arXiv preprints, not peer-reviewed journal articles — both are still legitimate to cite (LocateAnything-3B has no other citable source yet since it's brand new; Ultralytics never published a formal YOLOv8 paper at all), but it's worth a one-clause acknowledgement of that fact wherever you first cite them in Chapter 2, which the Literature Review draft already includes.
5. **Access dates** are set to today (21 August 2026) for the three web-only sources — update these to whatever date you actually do your final citation check, not the date I generated this list.
